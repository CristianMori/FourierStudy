Spectrum × Filter

Sampling & aliasing · Spectrum × Filter

A periodic signal is a sum of harmonics: x(t) = Σ Ak cos(2π k t / T + φk), for k = 0 … 64. Drag across the bars to draw the amplitudes Ak and phases φk; the waveform follows as you draw.

A filter is a second spectrum Hk: a gain and a phase shift for each harmonic. Filtering multiplies the two spectra bin by bin (amplitudes multiply, phases add), and the result is a new waveform. A phase of −90° turns a cosine into a sine; positive phase moves a harmonic earlier in time.

h(t) is the waveform whose spectrum is H. Multiplying spectra is the same as circular convolution in time: y(t) = (1/T) ∫ x(τ) h(t − τ) dτ over one period.

Interpolation kernels: joining samples with a kernel h is the same as filtering their spectrum by the Fourier transform of h, as in the reconstruction switch on the sampling page. Here the samples are Ts = T/(2k₀) apart, so their Nyquist frequency is bin k₀ and h(t) shows the kernel itself. Sharper kernels cut the images above k₀ better, but they ring: their negative lobes show up as 180° phase.
