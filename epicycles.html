Epicycles

Sampling & aliasing · Spectrum × Filter · Epicycles

Any closed curve is two periodic signals: its x position and its y position, one lap apart in time. Each signal is a Fourier series, a sum of rotating circles, so two chains of circles can draw the curve: one chain above the picture sets the pen's x, one beside it sets the pen's y. This page walks an image through every step. Each panel shows the result of the step before it.

1 · Image

Load a picture (PNG or JPG) or use the built-in sample. It is shrunk to at most 512 px and read as brightness. Nothing is uploaded: the file is read by your browser only.

2 · Vectorize into strokes

The image is blurred, then lines are found either as edges (Canny: Sobel gradient, thinning of the ridge, hysteresis threshold), good for photos and shapes, or as dark lines (pixels darker than the threshold), good for pen drawings and text. Either way the result is thinned to one pixel wide (Zhang–Suen), traced into polylines between endpoints and junctions, and simplified (Ramer–Douglas–Peucker). Short strokes are dropped.

3 · Connect into one closed path

A pen cannot lift, so the strokes are chained with bridges. They are ordered (nearest neighbour first, then 2-opt and Or-opt swaps) and each is entered from whichever end is closer, so the bridges stay short. Strokes are coloured by order along the path; the bridges are the dashed lines. The last stroke is joined back to the first.

4 · Two signals, x(t) and y(t)

The closed path is resampled at equal steps of arc length into N points, so t runs over one lap, the period T. Reading off the x and y coordinates gives two ordinary signals. The faint dashed curve is what is left of each signal when only the circles chosen in step 6 are kept.

5 · Fourier series of each signal

Each signal gets its own FFT, as a real signal, so x(t) = x₀ + Σ Ak cos(2π k t/T + φk) for k = 1 … N/2 and the same for y(t). Ak = 2|Xk|/N is the radius of one circle and φk = arg Xk is its starting angle; k is how many turns it makes per lap. The frequency and amplitude axes are logarithmic. The lit stems are the circles kept; grey ones are dropped.

6 · Circles on circles draw the image

Above the picture, the X chain: each circle is centred on the tip of the one before, largest first. Only the horizontal position of its tip matters, and that is the pen's x. Left of the picture, the Y chain is the same for y, turned a quarter turn so its tip's height is the pen's y. The two chains never share a circle; the dashed lines show each tip dropping onto the pen. Where a chain's circles leave its strip they are cut off.

Keeping fewer circles throws away the smallest ones, which carry the sharp corners and fine detail, so the drawing gets smoother and rounder: the same band-limiting as the anti-alias filter on the sampling page and the low-pass presets on the filter page.
